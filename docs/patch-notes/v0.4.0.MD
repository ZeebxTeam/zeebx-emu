# v0.4.0

An update for speed and polish. The Zeebo's processor is now recompiled to the computer's on every
system, the desktop got a new interface, and RetroArch and the handhelds got the settings they
were missing to run smoothly.

New:

- **New interface, in Qt**, which becomes the main one on Windows, Linux and macOS. The downloads
  page offers its installer first. The previous interface, in egui, still ships in the release,
  with the usual `zeebx` binary
- **ARM core recompiled everywhere.** Dynarmic replaces Unicorn as the emulator's processor. In
  the first measurement, Quake went from 52% to 170% of the console's speed
- **D-pad as stick**, an option per player, for games that only listen to the analog stick — like
  the Data East arcade games, among them Magical Drop III. It comes off, because some games read
  both (Zeeboids would move two squares per press). On desktop it is in the controls screen; in
  RetroArch, in `zeebx_dpad_to_analog_p1` and `_p2`; in `headless`, in `dpad_to_analog`
- **More complete save states**: they keep the whole 3D state, and the game clock restarts at the
  saved instant
- **More faithful MIDI music**: the soundfont plays at 44,100 Hz, no longer at 22,050, and with
  128 voices. The highs above 11 kHz are back, and a passage full of notes no longer swallows any
- **WebAssembly core**, for ROMBundler
- **iOS app**, unsigned. `zeebx-ios.zip` carries the IPA to install through AltStore, and
  `zeebx-ios-simulator.zip` runs in the Mac's Simulator. The games go in the app's `roms` folder.
  On the iPhone the processor is interpreted, not recompiled, because the system doesn't let the
  app generate code: expect less speed than on Android
- **Nintendo Switch core**, also for ROMBundler: it ships in the release as
  `zeebx_libretro-switch.zip`, the static library it links into the console package
- **Emulator log with five levels**, for those reporting bugs: `ZEEBX_LOG` on desktop, `zeebx_log`
  in RetroArch and `log` in the `headless` `config.ini`
- **Downloads page**, which offers the file for the visitor's device first
- **Choice of soundfont for MIDI music**, for those who have the console firmware's `.sf2` or
  prefer another one: in Settings › Audio on desktop, in Settings › Audio on Android, in the
  `zeebx_soundfont` option in RetroArch, and in `[audio] soundfont` or `--soundfont=` in
  `headless`. The `ZEEBX_SOUNDFONT` variable, which the "no soundfont" warning already told you to
  use, now works
- **Reverb and chorus in MIDI music**: the effect the music asks for on each instrument now
  sounds, and notes no longer end dry, as if they had been cut off. It comes on; to turn it off,
  use Settings › Audio on desktop, Settings › Audio on Android, the `zeebx_midi_efeitos` option in
  RetroArch or `[audio] midi_effects` in `headless`

Added compatibility with:

- Bejeweled Twist

In RetroArch and on the handhelds:

- **Speed limit**: 60 frames (the console's, default), 30 or off. Some games ran ten times faster
  on a machine with power to spare — Crash Nitro Kart reached 1,039%
- **Frame skip**, fixed or automatic, without changing the game's speed
- **Handheld profile**, with the internal resolution at 0.5x or 0.25x when drawing on the
  processor
- Rasterizer, internal resolution, antialiasing, anisotropic filtering, fog, volume and the MIDI
  engine become core options, and change without restarting the game
- GLES 3.0 on ARM64 devices and Mali GPUs
- The installer takes the whole package to ArkOS and muOS — core, ROMs, fonts, soundfont and the
  menu configuration —, and the core opens on ArkOS with an old glibc (R36S)

Android only:

- **Five save state slots** per game, which stay saved from one session to the next
- Game progress moves out of the app cache to a place of its own, and what a previous version
  saved is brought along
- The library tells you while it is looking for games

Bugs fixed:

- The Turma da Mônica voice lines were cut off right at the start, at just over half a second
- In `headless`, the controller chosen in `config.ini` only moved the stick: no button responded.
  Now it comes with the typical mapping of a controller, as on desktop, and the `config.ini` you
  already have starts working without changing anything besides the `controller` line
- Black screen in RetroArch in games that only draw in 2D, like Turma da Mônica and Zenonia
- Black image with antialiasing on
- Action buttons numbered out of the controller's order: 1 is the bottom one, 2 the left, 3 the
  top and 4 the right, and the mapping screen now says the same thing the game receives
- In RetroArch, D-pad as stick did nothing
- In RetroArch, the second game opened could ignore the options or close the program
- The Handheld profile didn't lower the resolution in some games
- Video drivers that can't scale the image ruined the frame with antialiasing or a higher
  resolution; now those settings turn themselves off on them
- Emulator closing when a game asked for an absurd block of memory
- The RetroArch log showed as errors what was only information
- On macOS, the `.dmg` for Macs with Apple chips (M1 or later) opened as "damaged". On those Macs,
  use the `macos-arm64` file
- The `.deb` didn't open on Ubuntu 24.04 without the full ALSA installed
